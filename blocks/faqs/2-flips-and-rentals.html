Flips and rentals

How is real estate bookkeeping different from regular small business bookkeeping?

Real estate books are set up property by property and flip by flip. Rentals need income and expenses tracked for each property, loan principal split from interest, and depreciation. Flips hold their costs on the balance sheet until the sale, then move them to cost of goods sold. A standard small business setup isn't built for either, which is why every REVI file is set up around your flips and properties.

Does a flip get depreciated?

No. Depreciation applies to property held to produce income. A flip is held for sale, so it doesn't depreciate. That's the reason flip books and rental books look nothing alike.

Are rehab costs deductible when you pay them?

Generally no. On a property held for sale, materials, labor, permits, and carrying costs get added to the cost of the property rather than deducted as paid. They come back as cost of goods sold when it sells.

Why do flip costs sit on the balance sheet?

Because until it sells, the property is inventory. If those costs hit the profit and loss as they happened, every month before the sale would show a loss and the closing month would show a windfall that isn't real.

Does a 1031 exchange apply to a flip?

No. A 1031 applies to property held for investment or productive use. A flip is held for sale, which puts it outside the rules. 1031 exchange documentation is a landlord service.

What's the difference between bookkeeping for a flip and bookkeeping for a rental?

A rental produces income every month, so its books are a profit and loss with a depreciation schedule. A flip produces nothing until it sells, so costs build up on the balance sheet and release all at once at closing. Running flips through rental-style books misstates every month in between.

Does loan principal show up on a rental's profit and loss?

No, only the interest. Principal reduces the loan balance on the balance sheet. Splitting the payment wrong makes a property look less profitable than it is.

What's the difference between a repair and a capital improvement?

A repair keeps the property in working condition and is deducted in the year it happens. An improvement adds value or extends useful life and gets depreciated over time.

Do I need separate books for flips and rentals?

Separate tracking at minimum. The two are taxed differently, and mixing them makes it harder to apply the right treatment to each. How far to take the separation, tracking versus separate entities, is a question for your CPA.
